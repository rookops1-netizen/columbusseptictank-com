Types of Septic Systems We Install

We install all types of septic systems including conventional gravity systems (the most common type, using gravity to move wastewater from tank to drain field), pressure distribution systems (using a pump to distribute effluent evenly), mound systems (for properties with shallow soil or high water tables), aerobic treatment units (advanced systems that provide additional treatment), and sand filter systems (for challenging site conditions). We assess your property's soil, slope, water table, and lot size to recommend the best system for your needs and budget.

Site Evaluation and Soil Testing

Before any septic system installation, a thorough site evaluation is essential. Our team assesses your property's soil type, slope, water table depth, and lot size. We conduct percolation tests to determine how quickly the soil absorbs water — this is critical for designing the drain field. We evaluate the depth to bedrock and seasonal high water table. We identify any site constraints like utility lines, wells, or water bodies that affect system placement. We work with local health departments to ensure the proposed system meets all regulatory requirements. This thorough evaluation ensures your septic system is designed for your specific property conditions and will provide reliable service for decades.

Conventional vs. Alternative Septic Systems

Not all properties can support a conventional gravity septic system. Properties with high water tables, shallow soil, poor drainage, or steep slopes may require alternative systems. Mound systems are built above ground level and are ideal for properties with shallow soil or high water tables. Aerobic treatment units use oxygen to promote bacterial growth and provide additional treatment — they're often required for properties near water bodies. Sand filter systems use a bed of sand to filter effluent before it reaches the drain field. Drip distribution systems deliver effluent slowly through a network of small tubes. Each system has specific advantages and costs. We'll recommend the best system for your property based on site conditions, budget, and regulatory requirements.

Permits and Regulations

Septic system installation is heavily regulated to protect public health and the environment. In Ohio, septic systems are regulated by local health departments under Ohio EPA guidelines. Permits are required for new installations, replacements, and major repairs. The permitting process typically involves site evaluation, system design approval, installation inspection, and final approval. We handle all aspects of the permitting process for our clients. We work closely with local health departments to ensure your system meets all requirements. We schedule and coordinate all required inspections. We provide all necessary documentation.
