Common Septic System Problems and Solutions

Septic systems can develop various problems over time. Baffle failure is one of the most common — the baffles that prevent solids from entering the drain field can deteriorate or break, allowing solids to pass through and clog the system. The solution is baffle replacement, which is typically a straightforward repair. Pipe damage from tree roots is another common issue — roots seek out the moisture and nutrients in septic pipes, growing into them and causing blockages. We use root cutting equipment and can install root barriers to prevent regrowth. Tank damage from settling or vehicle traffic can cause cracks that leak. Depending on the extent of damage, we may repair the crack or recommend tank replacement. Pump failure in systems with pumps or lift stations requires pump repair or replacement. Each problem has a specific solution, and our experienced technicians can diagnose and repair any issue.

Emergency Repairs: What to Do While Waiting

If you're experiencing a septic emergency, there are steps you can take while waiting for our technician to arrive. Stop using all water in your home — don't flush toilets, run sinks, take showers, or use washing machines or dishwashers. This prevents additional wastewater from entering the already stressed system. If you have a sewage backup, try to contain the mess and keep children and pets away from contaminated areas. If the backup is in a basement or lower level, move valuable items to higher ground. Open windows for ventilation if you smell sewage odors. If you know where your septic tank is, check for obvious signs of damage or overflow. Call us immediately and we'll dispatch a technician as quickly as possible.

Preventative Maintenance to Avoid Costly Repairs

The best way to avoid costly septic repairs is regular preventative maintenance. Pump your tank every 3-5 years to prevent solids from overflowing into the drain field. Have your system inspected annually to catch small problems before they become big ones. Use water efficiently to avoid overloading the system. Don't flush non-biodegradable items. Keep tree roots away from your septic system. Fix leaky faucets and toilets promptly. Use septic-safe cleaning products. Maintain your drain field by keeping vehicles and heavy equipment off it. Following these simple steps can extend the life of your septic system and save you thousands of dollars in repairs.
