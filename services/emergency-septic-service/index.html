Common Septic Emergencies

Septic emergencies can take many forms. Sewage backups into your home are the most urgent — raw sewage in your living space is a health hazard that requires immediate attention. Overflowing septic tanks can occur when the tank is full and waste has nowhere to go. Flooded drain fields can result from heavy rain or system failure, causing wastewater to surface in your yard. Broken or collapsed pipes can cause sewage to leak into the ground. Pump failures in systems with pumps or lift stations can cause backups. Damaged tank lids or risers can create safety hazards. Each emergency requires a specific response, and our team is trained to handle all types of septic emergencies quickly and effectively.

Frequently Asked Questions About Emergency Septic Service

How quickly can you respond to a septic emergency?

We typically respond within 2-4 hours for emergency calls. For life-threatening situations like raw sewage entering the home, we prioritize these calls and respond as quickly as possible.

Do you charge extra for emergency service?

Emergency service calls include an after-hours fee, but we believe in fair, transparent pricing. We'll provide the cost before beginning any work, and there are no hidden fees.

What should I do while waiting for emergency service?

Stop using all water in your home — don't flush toilets, run sinks, or use appliances. This prevents additional wastewater from entering the already stressed system. If you have a backup, try to contain the mess and keep children and pets away.
